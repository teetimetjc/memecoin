A touch is not a profit. The table above counts how often the cheap side reached a multiple of what it cost — nothing more. A take-profit that never triggers leaves the position held to settlement, and that is where a strategy like this bleeds. Both halves have to be counted before the idea means anything, which is what the panel above it is for.

Every price here is one you could actually get. Entries pay the ask, exits sell into the bid, and Kalshi's fee is charged twice — once going in, once coming out. Using a mid price on either side would invent an edge out of half a spread, and on contracts this cheap the spread is most of the supposed profit.

Sampling every 30 seconds undercounts. A level touched for ten seconds between two samples is missed. So any touch rate on this page is a floor, never an overstatement — but the same 30-second spacing also means a real order would need to be resting at the level, not placed by hand after noticing it.

This page shares no data with the v6 dashboard. That one scored a rule on who wins at settlement, and scored it wrongly for weeks. This is a separate question with a separate payload: not who wins, but whether the journey there is worth trading. Nothing here is a recommendation to bet.
