The dollar figures in these notes are written for $10 flat. The panels above follow the stake you picked; this commentary does not.

The firing rate is close to flat. Every hour gets between 17 and 40 of the signals — no hour carries more than about 5%. That’s expected: crypto trades continuously and the rule keys off order flow, not a US schedule. Which means the hourly profit swings are sample size, not skill. Twenty-four hours is twenty-four chances to find a dramatic-looking one; don’t schedule around them.

The grey band is the point, and it just got wider. These 853 bets are really only 298 independent windows: the rule reads the same market-wide flow on five tapes that move together, so it often bets all five coins the same way and they win or lose as one. Correcting for that, a rule with no edge could plausibly land anywhere within ±$1,431 — not the ±$665 the raw bet count implies. The blue line at +$521 sits well inside.

Why a losing hit rate can still profit. On 14 Sep the rule hit 49.0% and made +$191. Bets are not all the same size of swing: $10 at 33¢ buys 30 contracts and returns about $20 if it lands, while $10 at 67¢ buys 15 and returns about $5. Across all 853 bets the average win pays +$10.86 and the average loss costs −$10.36, so the true breakeven is about 48.8% — not the 49.6¢ average entry price. Comparing hit rate to the average price is the wrong test; P&L is the test.

That said, the EV interval is still −$1.06 to +$2.29 per bet. Losing money remains inside the range.

What splits the curve. The rule hits about 52% no matter what it pays, so it earns wherever the market charges less than that and loses wherever it charges more. Skipping entries at 60¢ or more lifts the total from +$546 to +$963 while staking $1,880 less, and it improves every one of the five coins — BTC flips from a loss to a gain. That consistency is the stronger argument: a real effect should show up on every tape, and it does. At 65¢ you are paying for a 64% chance from a rule that delivers about 51%. Nothing here is a recommendation to bet.

One trap the coin buttons make easy. Whichever coin looks best will look convincing. Pooling all 825 under-60¢ bets and dealing them at random into five fake “coins”, the best of the five averages 24% ROI by luck alone — and beats the real leader in more than 40% of runs. Best-of-five is not evidence. Use the buttons to check that the price effect holds everywhere, not to pick a coin.
